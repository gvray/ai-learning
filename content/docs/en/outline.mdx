## Learning & Acceptance Criteria

This course aims for understanding principles, hands-on practice, and independent problem-solving. Content is organized around each topic's characteristics — articles are not required to mechanically follow the same structure.

Core knowledge points should aim to cover the following aspects:

1. **Problem background**: Define the problem being solved, its context, and its practical value.
2. **Intuition & concepts**: Build a correct mental model through concrete examples; explain core concepts and their relationships.
3. **Principles & mechanisms**: Explain how things work and the design tradeoffs, using necessary math formulas, data flow, or system structure.
4. **Code & practice**: Understand the implementation through a minimal runnable example, and use mature frameworks or tools where appropriate.
5. **Experiment & analysis**: Verify key conclusions through reproducible experiments; observe how parameter changes affect results and why.
6. **Limitations & pitfalls**: Explain applicability conditions, common mistakes, failure modes, and easily confused concepts.
7. **Acceptance tasks**: Verify mastery of core knowledge through tasks such as explaining, deriving, implementing, or troubleshooting.

Different topics call for teaching approaches suited to their nature: math foundations emphasize concepts and derivation, model principles emphasize mechanisms and experiments, and engineering practice emphasizes implementation, testing, and troubleshooting.

**Mastery standard:** Reading completion is not the marker of learning completion. The primary basis is whether you can independently explain core principles, complete key implementations, analyze typical problems, and articulate technical tradeoffs.
